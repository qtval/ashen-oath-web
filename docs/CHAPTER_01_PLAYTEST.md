# Chapter One First-Time Story Test

## Purpose

This test decides whether Chapter One is ready for story lock. Testers must not read the README, project context, beat sheet, audit, or state schema before playing.

Do not explain unfamiliar names or correct a tester during play. Record confusion first; discuss it afterward.

## Observe without prompting

Record the scene and exact player comment whenever the tester:

- cannot explain where Garren is or what has physically changed;
- does not understand what a choice will risk immediately;
- chooses based on interface wording rather than character motive;
- forgets who holds Tavin, Orl's packet, the folio, strap, or warrant;
- expects an action that the story does not offer;
- feels that two choices are functionally identical;
- notices a character acting against an earlier relationship;
- cannot interpret the panel's primary story fact;
- encounters a term, name, or political fact that requires explanation.

## Ask immediately after the ending

1. What did Garren want when he reached the checkpoint?
2. What did Garren do during the war, and why does that matter here?
3. What did Tavin do during the twelve dark minutes? Do you trust him?
4. What does Meret want, and what is she afraid will happen?
5. What does Sella want, and what did she do to Tavin?
6. What evidence did your route find, and who possessed it at the end?
7. Why could revealing Red Hollow restart the war?
8. Why were the Bracken families in immediate danger?
9. Which choice was hardest? State the two costs you were weighing.
10. Which earlier choice visibly returned later?
11. What did your ending preserve, and what did it sacrifice?
12. What do you expect Garren to face next?

## Character response

For Garren, Meret, Tavin, Sella, and Perrin Dask, ask the tester for:

- three words describing the character;
- what the character publicly wanted;
- what the character might have concealed;
- one remembered action or line;
- whether the tester wants to meet the character again.

## Choice-quality rating

For each major choice, record:

- **Clear:** the known immediate consequences were understandable;
- **Compelling:** at least two options expressed believable competing motives;
- **Owned:** the result felt caused by the tester rather than selected from arbitrary labels;
- **Paid off:** the story later acknowledged the choice through access, dialogue, custody, harm, or ending text.

Any major choice receiving two or more failures from different testers blocks story lock.

## Panel-quality rating

For every displayed panel, ask only after the playthrough:

- What did you notice first?
- What fact or feeling did the image communicate?
- Did the image contradict the text or an earlier choice?
- Was the focal action readable on the phone screen?

A panel fails if its primary story fact is not recognized by most testers or if it visually restores an absent person, object, or consequence.

## Story-lock gate

Chapter One is eligible for story lock only when:

- unfamiliar testers complete it in the intended time range;
- they can explain Garren's goal, former work, the evidence chain, the threatened war, and the four principal characters' immediate motives;
- no repeated custody, geography, chronology, or cause-and-effect confusion remains;
- every major choice has an understood immediate cost and at least one visible later acknowledgment;
- no panel contradicts reachable story state;
- testers disagree about whom to trust for character reasons, not because information was missing;
- the chapter creates interest in replaying or continuing without requiring project lore.

Record failures in `docs/CHAPTER_01_AUDIT.md`. Repair repeated problems before producing final panels or beginning Chapter Two.
